Mixture-of-Experts,
all the way down

From “what even is an expert” to the load-balancing math that keeps a 671-billion-parameter model from eating itself. Every formula copied from the primary paper. Every number real and cited. No hand-waving in either direction.

Four rungs, and you stop where you like

Most MoE explainers pick a lane — a cartoon that leaves you unable to read a paper, or a wall of subscripts that never says why anyone bothered. This book refuses to pick. Every hard idea is climbed in four rungs:

Feel it The one-sentence intuition. Analogies only where they earn their keep — and always with the place they break.
How it works The mechanism, with a diagram. What moves, and where it goes.
The math The real equation from the real paper — with a plain-English gloss under every single one.
Research notes What the paper actually found, what's still contested, where the bodies are buried.

What you actually need, honestly: chapters 1–3 assume you know what a transformer feed-forward network is, and build everything else from there. Chapters 4 and 6 additionally assume you have seen a model run across more than one device — sharding, collectives, data and tensor parallelism. That second prerequisite went unstated for a while, and it is a real one.

Below the bar? Read 1, 5, 4, 6 first, then treat 2 and 3 as reference. MoE's motivation, its history and its systems consequences are genuinely accessible without an ML background; it is the routing math that is not, and it sits in chapters 2 and 3. That ordering came from a reader who didn't meet the prerequisite and finished anyway.

Writing MoE kernels for a living? This book is a synthesis, not research — it will not tell you anything new. For the kernel layer proper, go to MegaBlocks (dropless MoE, which chapter 4 does not cover) and DeepSeek's DeepEP. What chapter 6 offers you is a compact statement of the arithmetic, not an implementation.

The six chapters

  1. Every token pays full price The feed-forward network holds most of a transformer's parameters, and every token runs all of it. Conditional computation breaks that weld — and you'll derive Mixtral's 47B/13B yourself from ten config numbers. Read now · 16 min
  2. The router One small matrix has to make a discrete choice while staying differentiable. That tension explains almost everything that follows — softmax gating, top-k, noisy top-k, and why the gate value must multiply the output. Read now · 15 min
  3. Load balancing, and why experts collapse Left alone, a router picks favourites and the rich get richer. The fix is the most misunderstood equation in MoE — why fi · Pi works when neither factor alone does. Read now · 18 min
  4. Capacity, and the tokens thrown away Experts live in fixed-size buffers. Overflow one and your token is silently dropped — it skips the FFN and rides the residual to the next layer. Nothing errors. The loss just gets worse. Read now · 17 min
  5. The lineage: Shazeer → Switch → DeepSeek Seven years of the field arguing with itself, as a story. Each paper is a reaction to something specific that hurt about the last one. Ends with why 256 small experts beat 8 big ones. Read now · 19 min
  6. The systems reality The part architecture papers skip. Routing becomes a network operation — two all-to-all collectives in the critical path — and a sparse model has dense-model memory needs. Read now · 18 min

What this book promises

  • Every formula is copied from the primary paper, not from memory and not from another blog.
  • Every number is real and cited — where it comes from a paper. Where the book computes something itself, or assumes a hardware figure, it says so. Where a paper doesn't report something, the cell stays blank rather than getting a plausible guess.
  • Errors get fixed in public. An adversarial audit against the primary sources found 18 in the first complete draft, and a reader found a 7× hardware-spec error in chapter 6's calculator after that. Both are corrected; the corrections are in the git history.
  • Analogies are labelled as analogies, and each one is followed by where it stops being true.
